
Chosen operating point: b=64, r=2 bands/rows (of M=128 MinHash values).
Justification (head-of-product terms): a missed candidate causes only the
cheap "grumble" failure (never merged, permanently), while a false-positive
candidate threatens the 20-minute nightly budget (the catastrophic failure
mode that already killed a 31-hour job once). b=64,r=2 achieves 0% false
positive rate on labeled "different" pairs while still catching the
weakest true "same" pairs at 72.7-93.8% survival.

## Part E — Full-corpus skew + mitigation
See: e_report.txt (full Q2_E_REPORT.txt, generated by q2_e.py)

Baseline: 1,049,884 total candidate work units across 12,000 notices,
mean 87.49/notice, max 428/notice (max notice N008975, portal P241).
Chosen mitigation: ignore MinHash bands with document frequency > 10.
Result: work reduced to 1,005,846 (-4.19%), SAME recall unchanged at
0.9821 (274/279), DIFFERENT candidate rate unchanged at 0.0000.
Both baseline (1.48s) and mitigated (1.28s) retrieval comfortably within
the 1,200s (20-minute) nightly budget.

Additional analysis (not in the auto-generated report):
- Checked whether P001-P006 (nodal aggregators) are disproportionately
  represented in candidate work: they hold 38.9% of notices but only 32.0%
  of total work -- actually slightly UNDER-represented, not over. The
  skew (max=428 vs median=60, ~7x) is driven by individual heavily-
  republished notices (corrigenda + multi-portal republication chains),
  not by portal family.
- The mitigation's small effect (4.19%) is because no single band's
  document frequency exceeds 54 (out of 12,000 notices) -- the Part A
  shingle-level DF filter had already removed most of the boilerplate
  signal before MinHash signatures were even built, leaving little for
  a second band-level filter to catch.
